# Cooking for One: How Addme Makes Solo Meals Easy and Fun

Cooking for one person is harder than it sounds because most Nigerian recipes, market portions, and cooking habits are built around feeding a family. This leads to wasted vegetables, leftover protein, and the temptation to skip real meals altogether. The fix is not to cook less carefully. It is to shop and cook in the right portion size, and to use pre portioned meal packs like Addme where they make sense.

This guide walks through the real problems of solo cooking and simple ways to solve them.

## Why Cooking for One Is Harder Than It Looks

When you cook for a family, buying a full bag of pepper, a whole chicken, or a big bundle of vegetables makes sense because it gets used within a day or two. When you live alone, the same shopping list often means half the pepper goes bad in the fridge, or you eat the same soup for five days straight just to avoid waste.

This is not a personal failure. It is a portion size problem, and it is the same challenge that students, young professionals living alone, and people whose partners travel often all face.

## The Biggest Problems People Face When Eating Alone

Most solo cooking struggles fall into a few clear patterns.

### Food Waste

Fresh vegetables, tomatoes, and pepper are usually sold in quantities meant for a full pot of stew. Cooking for one means either eating the same dish for days or throwing away what you cannot finish before it spoils.

### The Cost of Small Portions

Buying small amounts of many different ingredients can sometimes cost more per meal than buying in bulk for a family, since you lose the benefit of shared cooking gas, time, and market prices.

### Low Motivation to Cook a Full Meal

After a long day, cooking a full pot of soup just for yourself can feel like too much effort for one plate. This is when many people fall back on skipping meals or eating something with little nutrition value.

### Repetition and Boredom

Without a plan, solo cooking tends to repeat the same two or three easy meals on a loop, which gets tiring fast and can lead to missing out on variety and nutrients.

## How to Shop and Cook Smarter for One Person

A few habits make a real difference once you start cooking mainly for yourself.

1. Buy proteins and vegetables in smaller, single use packs where possible, instead of market bulk sizes
2. Cook a base like rice, beans, or stew in a slightly larger batch, then portion and freeze extra servings
3. Keep a small stock of shelf stable meal mixes for days when you do not want to cook from scratch
4. Plan three or four rotating meals for the week instead of deciding fresh every single day
5. Use one pot meals to cut down on both cooking time and the number of dishes to wash

These steps reduce waste, save money over time, and take the daily decision of what to cook off your plate, literally and mentally.

## Easy One Pot Meals for One Person

One pot meals are the easiest way to cook for yourself without extra waste or extra washing up. A few reliable options include:

1. Noodles with an egg and any vegetables you have on hand
2. Jollof rice made in a small pot with just enough for one or two servings
3. Pasta with a simple sauce and whatever protein is in the fridge
4. Beans porridge, which also keeps well in the fridge for a day or two

The common thread is that each of these can be made in one pot, in a single portion, without needing to buy extra ingredients that will go to waste.

## Smart Storage Tips for a Solo Kitchen

How you store food matters as much as how you shop for it when you live alone.

1. Keep dried packs like Noodlemate, Pastamate, Ricemate, and Mealmate in a cool, dry cupboard, since they do not need refrigeration until cooked
2. Store leftover cooked rice or stew in small, single serving containers rather than one large pot, so you only reheat what you plan to eat
3. Freeze extra portions of soup or stew in flat bags so they take up less space and thaw faster
4. Keep a small supply of eggs on hand, since they last for weeks in the fridge and can turn almost any base meal into a more complete one
5. Label leftovers with the date you cooked them, so nothing sits forgotten at the back of the fridge

Good storage habits mean you always have something ready to cook, even on days when you have no energy to think about a full meal plan.

## Tips to Keep Solo Meals From Getting Boring

Eating the same thing every day is one of the fastest ways to lose interest in cooking for yourself. A few small changes keep meals interesting without much extra work.

1. Rotate between Noodlemate, Pastamate, and Ricemate so the base of the meal changes even when prep stays simple
2. Add one fresh element each time, such as a sliced tomato, a boiled egg, or a squeeze of lemon
3. Try a new seasoning or sauce occasionally instead of the same one every week
4. Treat one meal a week as a chance to cook something you would not normally make just for one person

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it cheaper to cook for one person or to buy pre portioned meal packs?

It depends on the ingredient. Fresh vegetables and proteins bought in small amounts can sometimes cost more per meal due to waste, while pre portioned packs like Addme meal mixes give you a fixed cost per meal with nothing left to spoil.

### How do I stop wasting vegetables when cooking for one?

Buy smaller quantities more often, freeze what you will not use within two or three days, or switch to dried vegetable mixes that do not spoil quickly and can be stored for longer periods.

### What is the easiest meal to cook for just one person?

Noodles, rice, or pasta with a simple protein and vegetable mix are usually the fastest and easiest options for one person, especially when using a pre portioned pack that removes the need to measure or prep separate ingredients.

### Can I freeze single portions of rice or stew?

Yes, rice and most stews freeze well for later use. Cook a slightly larger batch, cool it fully, then portion it into single serving containers before freezing, so you always have a backup meal ready.
